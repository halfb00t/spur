<objective>
The three failure branches: what broke, who was refused, and which worker was replaced.

Plan 03-01 made a successful request observable. This plan finishes the vocabulary with
the branches that matter at 3am: `build.failed` at the three `except` clauses in
`app.model()` with the exception class and the duration the client actually waited;
`queue.refused` at the single admission refusal in `_build_slot()` with the in-flight
count and the ceiling it hit; and `worker.replaced` from inside `pool.recreate_for`'s
identity guard, with the hash slot and whether the cause was a timeout or a dead worker.

Purpose: `/api/health` already exposes a `workers_replaced` count (Phase 2 D-13), but a
count answers "how many" and never "when" or "why" — the log is the only place the *when*
can live (D-08). Likewise a `503` in an access line does not say whether the service was
saturated or a worker had died. After this plan, the incident question
`PROJECT.md` sets as success metric #3 is answerable from the stderr stream alone: for one
request, was it refused, served from cache, compressed or built; if built, how long; if it
failed, which class; and was a worker replaced.

Output: the remaining three of D-17's five per-event helpers, their call sites in
`app.py` and `pool.py`, and `caplog` branch tests in `tests/test_api.py` and
`tests/test_pool.py`.
</objective>

<flagged_assumptions>
1. **`build.failed` does not carry the hash slot, and that is deliberate.** CONTEXT.md
   D-06 describes the parent's `except` branches as seeing the exception class "plus the
   hash slot". They do not: the slot is `hash(p) % self.workers`, arithmetic that lives in
   `pool.executor_for`, and `app.model()` reaches the pool only through the injected
   `build_backend` seam (Phase 2 D-15) precisely so it never touches pool internals — the
   same boundary that keeps the serving process kernel-free (Phase 2 D-02). Reaching
   `app.state.pool.workers` from an `except` clause would also be wrong under test, where
   the backend is overridden and no pool exists. The slot therefore rides on
   `worker.replaced`, emitted from `pool.py` where the arithmetic actually is; a failed
   build and the replacement it triggered are correlated by adjacency in the stream and by
   the `cause` field. Surfaced rather than silently dropped.

2. **`worker.replaced` carries no `request` correlation id.** D-13 mints the id as a plain
   local in `app.model()` and passes it to the records that request emits; threading it
   into the pool would mean changing `BuildPool.export`'s signature and with it the
   `BuildBackend` type the CLI-shared path depends on. The preceding `build.failed` record
   carries both the request id and the exception class, and `worker.replaced` follows it
   within microseconds carrying the slot — which is what an incident reader needs.
   Accepted limitation, not an oversight.
</flagged_assumptions>
